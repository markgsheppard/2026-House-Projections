Notes & method

What is being fit

The forecast’s own equation, with its dials free. Each House district’s predicted margin is its lean at an even national vote plus elasticity times the environment, plus the mail-in and economic shocks for its state, plus the fundraising adjustment times its weight; each Senate race starts from the margin implied by its frozen odds and moves with the environment relative to the generic ballot the odds were set at. The scored races are every House and Senate contest with a Democrat-minus-Republican margin in the count (an independent stands in for a missing or outpolled major-party candidate, as in Nebraska and Alaska); California’s same-party races and the uncontested seats are left out. Races still being counted are scored on their margin so far, weighted by the share of the expected vote that is in.

Margins, robust fits a Huber loss, in which a miss beyond eight points counts linearly rather than squared, so the handful of races whose model inputs are plainly wrong cannot steer the dials; Margins fits the root mean square miss; Winners fits the log loss of the model’s own win probabilities against who is ahead, which cares about calling races right and being confident about it. Nelder-Mead is run from the current dials and seven random settings, the best answer is snapped to the dials’ steps and polished by coordinate search. With state effects on, a ridge-penalized offset for every state is solved in closed form inside each evaluation, and the cross-validated figures re-estimate each state’s offset without the race being scored, so they show what the effects would have been worth before the fact. Tune by cross-validation picks the penalty with the lowest leave-one-out miss.

Reading it

Nothing here is a forecast. The dials are fit after the results are in, so the best settings describe the election that happened and are a yardstick for the model, not a method that would have found them in advance. A set of six national dials cannot call every race; what is left in Still wrong is idiosyncratic to those contests. State effects can take the fit much further in sample, and because the economy shock is itself a state-level term, free state effects make the economy dial unidentified: the ridge penalty is what lets the two share the work, and the cross-validated numbers are the honest ones.

On the economy dial, the threshold makes the loss a staircase in that direction, which is why the heat map is drawn at fixed steps and why a fit can land on a plateau; the profile button, which refits everything else at each size of the shock, is the better guide to how much the dial matters. Shock falls on re-aims the term: at the governor’s party as the model was built, at the president’s party as a national-incumbent penalty, or at Democrats as a plain under-performance in stressed states.
